Heat Meds Evidence

Some everyday medicines make hot days more dangerous.

Water pills, some blood pressure pills, some antidepressants, and others change how your body handles heat. Most people are never told. This page checks your medicines against the CDC's heat guidance and this week's heat forecast where you live, then gives you one printable plan to go over with your pharmacist.

Try an exampleMom, 74, in Phoenix: a water pill, a blood pressure pill, an antidepressant, insulin, and a cholesterol pill.

Free. No account. We don't store anything. To check your list, medicine names are looked up in the National Library of Medicine's RxNorm and your ZIP goes to the National Weather Service.

Type each name as it appears on the bottle, brand or generic, then press Add. If we are not sure what you meant, we ask before adding it.

    Add at least one medicine to see a plan.

    Without a ZIP you still get the medicine check, just not this week's heat.

    Who this is for

    Older adults and the people who look after them. It is meant for the kitchen table with the pill bottles out, or for the pharmacy counter. It is not a substitute for your pharmacist or doctor, and it never tells anyone to stop a medicine.

    Why it matters

    In 2025 the CDC asked clinicians to go over heat risks from medicines with their patients: which classes dull thirst, reduce sweating, or lower blood pressure in heat, which combinations add up, and which medicines spoil in a hot car. Patients had no tool to do this themselves. This one uses the CDC's own table, quoted line by line, joined to the National Weather Service's HeatRisk forecast for your ZIP.

    What you get

    • Seven days of heat risk for your ZIP, each day with a color and a word.
    • One card per medicine that matters in heat: why, what to watch for, and one question to ask your pharmacist. Every card carries the CDC's exact words.
    • The CDC's combination warning when it applies, and storage notes for medicines that spoil in heat.
    • An honest list of the medicines you entered that the CDC guidance does not list.
    • A printable page for the fridge or the pharmacist.

    How well does it work? The Evidence page shows the results of running the 300 most-prescribed US medicines through this site, compared with the UK's and Canada's guidance.
